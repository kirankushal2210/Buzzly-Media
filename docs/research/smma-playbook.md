# What a social media agency needs: research and Buzzly's gap plan

Researched 24 November 2026. This is a first pass at web research. Most sources are vendor or agency blogs, so prices and percentages are **asking prices or self-reported figures, not market data**. Check anything we rely on (especially legal dates) against the primary source.

---

## 1. How established agencies run

Successful agencies look different from the outside, but underneath they run the same five systems:

| System | What it does | What good looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Get clients** | Lead flow and sales | Referrals, a free audit as a lead magnet, case studies, qualification before calls |
| **Sell** | Proposals and pricing | Three fixed-scope monthly tiers, ad spend billed separately, 3–12 month terms |
| **Onboard** | Contract to first post | Contract, deposit, questionnaire, access handover, brand audit; about 3–4 weeks to the first scheduled post |
| **Deliver** | Plan, make, approve, publish | A content calendar, one approval path per client, a scheduling tool, a shared asset library |
| **Report and retain** | Show results, renew | A monthly report built from real account data, a next-month plan, a quarterly review |

Sources: [Sked: client onboarding process](https://skedsocial.com/blog/social-media-client-onboarding-process-checklist-questionnaire), [PostPlanify: onboarding checklist](https://postplanify.com/blog/social-media-agency-client-onboarding-checklist), [SocialPilot: agency SOPs](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/sops-for-social-media-agency-automation), [AgencyAnalytics: getting clients](https://agencyanalytics.com/blog/get-more-clients).

## 2. The tool stack agencies use

The typical agency runs **7–9 tools**. The advice is to start with a small core and add tools only when there's a real need ([Forapost](https://forapost.online/blog/agencies/agency-tech-stack-for-social-media-management-in-2026-what-you-actually-need), [Flockler](https://flockler.com/blog/social-media-marketing-tools-for-agencies-2026), [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/social-media-marketing-tools), [Quimby Digital](https://quimbydigital.com/best-social-media-marketing-tools-2026/)).

| Layer | Commonly used | Starting point for Buzzly |
|---|---|---|
| Scheduling and inbox | Sprout Social (large teams), Agorapulse, SocialPilot, Hootsuite, Zoho Social | One agency-tier scheduler with client approval links (compare SocialPilot, Agorapulse and Zoho Social on price in INR) |
| Design | Canva, Figma | Canva Teams with Buzzly and client brand kits |
| Video | CapCut, Premiere / DaVinci, Remotion (already in use for our promos) | Keep the Remotion pipeline for branded motion; use one editor for shoots |
| Reporting | Looker Studio (social + GA4 + ads), the scheduler's own reports | Looker Studio template per client |
| Listening / creators | Brand24, Brandwatch, HypeAuditor | Later, once a client needs it |
| Automation | Zapier, Make | Later: lead to CRM, approvals to Slack or WhatsApp |
| Projects and CRM | ClickUp / Notion / HubSpot | One workspace: pipeline, client boards, SOPs |

## 3. How AI is being used

Agencies use AI in three places: **drafting** (captions, calendars, hook variations), **reporting** (first drafts of monthly summaries from real account data) and **routine replies**. Every source stresses that a human must review before anything is published. Sources warn that AI summaries "sound confident but misstate the numbers", and that health, finance and legal clients need review at every stage. Productivity claims, such as two people running 15 accounts, are vendor marketing and unverified ([Sociality](https://sociality.io/blog/ai-social-media-reporting/), [Apaya](https://apaya.com/blog/ai-social-media-automation-for-agencies), [Mallary](https://mallary.ai/blog/how-social-media-agencies-use-ai-in-2026), [AgencyGrowth](https://agencygrowth.com/ai-agents-for-social-media-agencies)).

**What this means for Buzzly:** this is the basis for the "AI marketing" section we planned to keep off the landing page. The honest pitch is "AI speeds up our drafts and reports; a person checks everything".

## 4. Pricing in India (asking prices)

- Small business: about ₹15,000–40,000 per month. Boutique organic and Reels work: about ₹35,000–75,000 per month. Mid-market and enterprise: ₹50,000 to ₹1,50,000+ per month. Full-stack, multi-channel work: ₹5,00,000+ per month ([Beetle Dynamics](https://beetledynamics.com/blog/social-media-marketing-cost-india/), [Creative Agencys](https://creativeagencys.com/social-media-agency-pricing-cost-india-2026), [SocialSaga](https://socialsaga.in/blog/social-media-marketing-cost-india), [GrowthKul](https://growthkul.com/blogs/digital-marketing-retainer-pricing), [LifeDesigner](https://www.lifedesigner.io/blog/social-media-marketing-packages-india)).
- Agencies price by **output** (number of reels, carousels, platforms, shoots, community management, reporting). Ad spend is billed on top, and **18% GST** applies. Terms of 3–12 months are common, with 6 months most common.
- **What this means for Buzzly:** the three tiers we already have (Launch Sprint, Growth Engine, Full-Stack Partner) match how the market sells. They still have **no prices or deliverable counts**, which the CEO needs to decide. We should not publish numbers until then.

## 5. Legal and compliance (India)

- **Influencer and creator content (ASCI and CCPA):** every paid or gifted creator post needs a clear disclosure label. **Both the brand and the agency** share responsibility, so creator contracts should include disclosure clauses. There are stricter rules for **health and finance** creators (relevant to Buzzly Health), and newer guidance covers AI-generated or virtual influencers. ASCI's own review found that most influencer ads it examined needed changes ([ASCI](https://www.ascionline.in/social), [Sansa Legal](https://www.sansalegal.com/post/asci-influencer-advertising-guidelines-2026-disclosure-rules-for-paid-content-and-ai-influencers), [Medianews4u](https://www.medianews4u.com/asci-report-69-of-indias-top-digital-stars-fail-to-meet-influencer-advertising-guidelines/)). Check the current text on ascionline.in before we write our process.
- **Data protection (DPDP Act and Rules, notified November 2025):** most consent, notice and breach duties apply from about **May 2027**, and Consent Manager registration starts around November 2026. An agency is usually the client's **data processor**, which needs a written contract. For its **own** leads it is the data owner (the "fiduciary"). Consent must be specific and opt-in (no pre-ticked boxes), and bought or scraped lead lists are a liability. TRAI rules on SMS and WhatsApp marketing already apply ([dpdp-compliance.in](https://dpdp-compliance.in/industries/media-advertising), [Pantheraa](https://pantheraa.com/blog/dpdp-consent-lead-forms.html), [Cyberdunes](https://www.cyberdunes.com/blog/news/dpdp-consent-manager-deadline), [Digital Hangover](https://digitalhangover.in/blog/dpdp-act-for-marketers/)). Exact dates differ between sources, so check the Gazette.
- **What this means for Buzzly:** our contact form is already opt-in and links to the privacy policy. Before launch we need a lawyer-reviewed client contract (scope, revisions, ownership of content, termination, NDA, data-processing clause) and a creator agreement with disclosure clauses.

## 6. Winning clients

- Getting clients is the most-cited problem for agencies (37% call it their top challenge in [AgencyAnalytics' survey](https://agencyanalytics.com/blog/get-more-clients)).
- **Referrals** are the cheapest channel. One unverified estimate says 40–60% of a niche agency's new clients come from referrals ([PostPlanify](https://postplanify.com/blog/how-to-find-social-media-marketing-agency-clients)).
- A **free audit** is the standard lead magnet ([GAIN](https://blog.gainapp.com/?p=3017), [NEWMEDIA](https://newmedia.com/blog/social-media-lead-generation)). We already have one, the Content Engine Score.
- **Case studies with real results** close deals. Ours are still samples, so the first real client stories are the single biggest upgrade.
- Directories matter in India: [Clutch](https://clutch.co/in/agencies/social-media-marketing/hyderabad?page=2) lists over 300 Hyderabad agencies, and [DesignRush](https://www.designrush.com/agency/social-media-marketing/in/hyderabad) has a Hyderabad ranking (some DesignRush placements are paid). A verified Clutch profile with reviews is worth getting once we have clients.

## 7. What works on each platform (India)

The sources agree:
- **Instagram Reels** for consumer brands (food, skincare, real estate).
- **YouTube Shorts** for searchable explainers (fits health and real estate).
- **LinkedIn** video for business-to-business clients.
- Each platform needs its own edit rather than the same cut reposted, a strong first-second hook, on-screen text for viewers watching on mute, and about three good videos a week.

Sources: [EICT IIT Kanpur](https://www.eicta.iitk.ac.in/knowledge-hub/digital-marketing/short-video-marketing-strategy-2026), [Webfluence](https://webfluence.in/the-brief/short-form-video-strategy-indian-brands-2026), [DecodeGrowth](https://decodegrowth.in/blogs/youtube-shorts-vs-reels-vs-linkedin-video-brands-2026/), [Distk](https://distk.in/blog/short-form-video-b2b-india-reels-shorts-2026.html). The statistics in these posts are unverified.

## 8. Buzzly today vs. what an agency needs

| Need | Buzzly today | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| Portfolio site | Built: landing page, services, clients, contact form that sends, light/dark modes | Real clients, business email, social links, team photos, go live |
| Lead magnet | Content Engine Score | Will be moved off the landing page as planned |
| Tiers / pricing | Three named tiers, no prices | CEO decides prices and deliverables |
| CRM / pipeline | Enquiries arrive by email (Resend) | A simple pipeline (sheet, Notion or HubSpot free tier) |
| Proposal and contract | None | Proposal template, contract reviewed by a lawyer, creator agreement |
| Onboarding | None | Questionnaire, access checklist, kickoff agenda |
| Production | Remotion promo pipeline, shoot capability | Content calendar template, approval flow, asset library |
| Scheduling | None | One agency scheduler |
| Reporting | None | Looker Studio monthly report template |
| Client portal | Prototype exists in the site (not linked from the home page) | Decide: build it out, or use the scheduler's client view |
| Compliance | Opt-in form, privacy page | ASCI disclosure process, DPDP processor clause |
| Social proof | Sample stories only | First 2–3 real, approved client stories |

## 9. Suggested order once the portfolio is live

1. **Business basics (no code):** prices for the three tiers, the business email, Instagram and LinkedIn accounts, and the client contract and creator agreement (lawyer review).
2. **Operations kit (documents and templates):** proposal template, onboarding questionnaire, content calendar, approval flow, monthly report template. These can be docs and sheets; no software needs building.
3. **Tool choices:** choose one scheduler, Canva Teams, and Looker Studio; set up a pipeline/CRM.
4. **First clients:** referrals plus the free audit; turn each into a real case study with written approval.
5. **Website follow-ups:** move the sprint features, build out the technology section, then the AI marketing section.

Steps 2 and 3 are where research can go deeper next: compare schedulers by INR price and approval features, and draft the templates.
